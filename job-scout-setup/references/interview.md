# Interview questions

Ask in rounds of up to 4. Skip anything the CV already answers. Every question can be skipped. Offer options the person can click where possible. Adapt the examples to their field and level: a policy graduate, a nurse and a senior engineer need different examples.

## Round 1: what and where
1. What roles are you looking for? Up to 5, best first. (Offer examples drawn from the CV, e.g. "policy officer", "parliamentary assistant", "data analyst", "project manager NGO".)
2. Level and contract: internship / traineeship / entry level / experienced / senior / student job; full-time / part-time (hours a week) / fixed-term / freelance. Which are acceptable?
3. Location: city and commuting limit. Remote or hybrid OK? Would you relocate, and where?
4. Earliest and latest start date.

## Round 2: limits and deal-breakers
5. Minimum salary, if any. (Optional; only used to flag postings below it.)
6. Hard no's: what makes a job worthless to you? (Examples: unpaid, full-time only, shift work, sales targets, lots of travel, a specific industry, needs a driving licence.)
7. Work authorization: do you need a visa or permit sponsored there? If you already have the right to work, should applications mention it, and in which words?
8. Languages and honest level for each.

## Round 3: honesty and history
9. What do employers in your field often require that you do NOT have? (Tools, degrees, licences, security clearance, years of experience.) Mandatory ones cap the score; "nice to have" ones are fine.
10. Anything the AI must never claim about you? (E.g. "never say I led a team", "my French is basic", exact numbers for your results.)
11. Where have you already applied, with what result? (A list, a spreadsheet, or "check my Gmail".) Employers to exclude, e.g. your current one?
12. Employers or organisations you especially want watched?
13. Do deadlines matter in your field (public sector, EU/UN, NGOs, academia, traineeship windows)? Any fixed annual application windows you know of?

## Round 4: how it should run
14. Which parts: the scout (recommended), the browser sweep (needs your computer on; reads LinkedIn with your account, which LinkedIn may restrict), speculative outreach drafts?
15. Time zone, how often (3 times a week or every weekday), and notifications by phone, email or both?
16. Report language, and language(s) for outreach emails.
17. Outreach only: what kind of organisations to target (size, sector, city), the phone number and one link (portfolio, LinkedIn, website) to include, and optionally a short email you wrote yourself so drafts can match your tone.

## What not to ask
- ID, passport, social security, bank or card numbers
- Health, religion, family plans, or anything not needed to score a job
- Things the CV already answers
